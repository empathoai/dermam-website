## Overview

DERMA.M is a premium, image-led, monochromatic website system for an aesthetic skin health center in West Palm Beach. The interface must feel refined, editorial, calm, precise, and conversion-oriented without becoming clinical, cold, or generic.

The visual strategy is not “gray website.” It is a restrained grayscale UI where photography creates warmth and emotional harmony. Skin tones, treatment hands, product textures, towels, soft shadows, IV bags, treatment rooms, and team imagery carry the human premium feeling. Grays exist as contrast, depth, borders, and surfaces — not as large filler backgrounds.

The system must support multiple page types without changing visual language: Homepage, Tier 1 landings, treatment hubs, Tier 3 treatment pages, Booking, Contact, About/Nosotros, and IV Therapy. Every page should feel like part of the same DERMA.M ecosystem.

The approved Homepage is the visual quality baseline for the whole site. It is not a literal template to copy everywhere. Future pages and components should extract its principles: off-white page rhythm, white content cards, controlled charcoal contrast blocks, editorial image treatment, strong typographic hierarchy, refined card/background contrast, subtle founder/team motion, a dark FinalCTA treatment, and a dark premium footer.

The main design posture is luxury through restraint: generous whitespace, strong typographic hierarchy, large rounded containers, calm monochrome contrast, precise image placement, shallow shadows, subtle borders, and clear conversion paths.

DERMA.M must not look like a hospital, generic clinic, SaaS page, low-end med spa template, beauty salon catalog, or gray prototype.

Primary visual principles:

1. Use full-bleed editorial image heroes.
2. Use Poppins for the entire interface hierarchy.
3. Use Hamilton only as a controlled H1 accent.
4. Use image-led cards and split sections.
5. Use large radius as a signature shape language.
6. Use light editorial surfaces by default.
7. Use dark sections only for hero, trust, protocols, final CTA, footer, or controlled proof/contrast moments.
8. Keep conversion clear but never visually aggressive.
9. Preserve approved Homepage sections during consistency passes; improve surrounding pages toward the same system, not by redesigning approved work.

## Colors

DERMA.M uses a strict grayscale system. The grayscale palette is functional, not decorative. Each token has a role in contrast, hierarchy, surface separation, or image readability.

The default site canvas is `background` / `#f2f0f1`. It is the dominant off-white page surface, not pure white. This prevents the site from feeling sterile while staying clean and premium.

The primary content surface is `surface` / `#ffffff`. Use pure white for treatment cards, forms, proof cards, FAQ items, booking/contact panels, and contained content surfaces. Do not use pure white as a repeated dominant full-page background; white should read as content placed over the off-white canvas.

The main dark surface is `primary` / `#141313`. Use it for the Hero, TrustBar dark mode, protocol contrast blocks, final CTA, footer, and selected proof/trust moments that need intentional contrast. Dark sections should be deliberate and limited. Do not alternate dark/light sections randomly.

`surface-soft` / `#e6e4e4` is a utility surface. It may be used for shallow separators, subtle content panels, or contained surfaces. It should not dominate the page, become filler, or replace photography as the visual harmonizer.

Light mode contrast:

- Primary text uses `text-primary` / `#141313`.
- Secondary copy uses `text-secondary` / `#4e4d4d`.
- Muted copy, captions, and metadata use `text-muted` / `#666666`.
- Borders use `border` / `#e6e4e4` or `border-strong` / `#cccaca`.
- Cards use `surface` / `#ffffff`.

Dark mode contrast:

- Primary text uses `text-inverse` / `#f2f0f1`.
- Secondary text uses `text-inverse-secondary` / `#cccaca`.
- Borders use `border-dark` / `rgba(255,255,255,0.10)`.
- Glass cards use `rgba(255,255,255,0.04)` to `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`.

Hero overlays are mandatory on image backgrounds. Use a left-to-right dark gradient so copy remains readable without damaging the image composition. The text side should receive the strongest overlay; the visual side can remain more photographic.

Non-hero image background sections should use softer overlays than heroes so the photography remains present. If light text sits over an image, solve contrast locally with a dark glass/panel or a targeted overlay rather than washing out the whole photograph. If a light card sits over an image, keep the overlay soft enough that the image still contributes mood and warmth. Do not use aggressive CSS to rescue a bad image; replace or recrop the image when the photograph cannot support the section.

Never introduce saturated accent colors unless approved. Avoid medical blue, green, pink, gold, neon, or gradient accent systems. IV Therapy imagery may include fruit and wellness colors inside photography, but the UI surrounding it must remain monochromatic.

Interactive states must remain subtle. On hover, cards may slightly darken borders, gently scale images, or move chevrons. Buttons may invert or shift background slightly. Do not use colored hover states.

## Typography

DERMA.M uses Poppins as the primary typographic system and Hamilton as a single controlled emotional accent. Typography must create hierarchy without making the site loud.

Major display text uses uppercase. Reading text, cards, buttons, links, and FAQ use sentence case. This creates contrast between editorial authority and calm readability.

Uppercase is allowed only for:

1. Hero display titles.
2. Page title roles.
3. Main section heading roles when using `heading-section`.
4. Eyebrows and labels.
5. Stats labels.
6. Editorial numbers such as `01`, `02`, `03`.
7. Metadata micro-labels.

Sentence case is required for:

1. Card titles.
2. Treatment titles inside cards.
3. Supporting copy.
4. Body paragraphs.
5. CTAs and buttons.
6. Editorial links.
7. FAQ questions.
8. Booking option titles.
9. Contact channel titles.
10. Trust card titles.

Title Case is not the default. Do not capitalize every word unless the phrase is a proper name, brand term, or treatment name that requires it.

Hamilton is allowed only inside a Hero H1 and in approved major editorial compositions such as the Home FinalCTA. It is not a general heading font, not a card font, not a logo replacement, and not a decorative system. The approved Hero pattern is a single inline word such as `tu` inside the Hero display title.

Approved Home Hero pattern:

```txt
SALUD PROFESIONAL PARA tu PIEL
```

Hamilton rules:

1. Use only once per Hero, or once in an explicitly approved major editorial CTA composition.
2. Use inside the H1 display composition by default; the approved Home FinalCTA is the only documented CTA exception.
3. Use normal-case, never uppercase.
4. Use light weight.
5. Scale relative to the surrounding display type, approximately `1.32em-1.56em` depending on the approved composition.
6. Vertically adjust so it integrates into the display line.
7. Never use in cards, nav, body, FAQ, forms, treatment titles, ordinary CTAs, stats, or footer. Do not expand Hamilton usage without explicit approval.

The Hero H1 uses `display-hero` on Homepage and `display-hero-compact` on internal heroes. Section headings use `heading-section`. Cards use `title-card`. Paragraphs use `body-md` or `body-lg` depending on prominence.

The AI or builder must not style generic `h1`, `h2`, or `h3` globally without component context. Use semantic design roles instead: `HeroTitle`, `SectionTitle`, `CardTitle`, `Eyebrow`, `BodyCopy`, `ButtonLabel`, `StatNumber`, `StatLabel`.

## Layout

The layout system is editorial and spacious. It must avoid dense grids, crowded cards, and generic template spacing.

Default container width is `1280px` with horizontal padding from `container-x`. Most sections should use this global container. Text-heavy blocks should use `text-narrow` / `48rem` to maintain readability.

Default desktop section spacing is `section-y` / `6rem`. Compact utility sections may use `4rem`. Mobile sections use `section-y-mobile` / `4rem`.

The Homepage hero uses a minimum height of `86vh`. Internal hero heights vary by page type:

- Tier 1 landing pages: `72vh`.
- Hub pages: `68vh`.
- About page: `72vh`.
- Booking and Contact: `50–58vh`.
- IV Therapy: `60vh`.
- Tier 3 treatment pages: `60–68vh`.

All Hero sections are full-bleed editorial systems. Do not create split heroes with image on one side and text on the other. The image or video always covers the full Hero container with `object-fit: cover`, and text sits over the image with a mandatory overlay.

Grid behavior:

- Featured treatment cards use 3 columns on desktop and 1 column on mobile.
- Hub category cards use 4 columns on desktop, 2 columns on tablet if readable, and 1 column on mobile for major cards.
- TrustBar uses 4 columns on desktop, 2x2 on tablet, and 1 column or compact 2x2 on mobile depending available width.
- Split sections use two columns on desktop and one column on mobile.
- Booking and contact layouts use split cards on desktop and stacked cards on mobile.

Large rounded section containers should use `rounded.section` / `2.5rem`. Treatment cards use `rounded.card` / `2rem`. Images inside cards usually use `rounded.image` / `1.5rem` unless the image is flush at the top of an overflow-hidden card.

Do not use large flat gray blocks as layout filler. If a section feels visually empty, solve it with image placement, spacing, hierarchy, or card composition — not by adding a heavy gray background.


Section rhythm should feel editorial and intentional. The dominant page rhythm is off-white canvas with white content cards or contained panels. Dark charcoal blocks are reserved for strategic contrast: heroes, final CTAs, footer, trust/proof, or protocol sections. Transitions between sections should be handled through spacing, surface contrast, image composition, and typographic hierarchy, not random background alternation. A visual consistency pass must harmonize sections toward the approved Homepage system without cloning the Homepage hero or redesigning approved sections.
## Components

### HeroSection — Full-Bleed Editorial System

Purpose: establish positioning, authority, emotional tone, and conversion direction.

Internal hierarchy:

1. Visual container.
2. Media background.
3. Dark overlay.
4. Social proof pill.
5. Eyebrow.
6. Hero title.
7. Hamilton accent span.
8. Supporting paragraph.
9. Hero CTA Group.
10. Optional TrustBar sibling.

Token mapping:

- Container background: `primary` when image is loading or unavailable.
- Media: full-bleed image/video with `object-fit: cover`.
- Overlay: `overlay-dark-start`, `overlay-dark-mid`, `overlay-dark-end`.
- Eyebrow: `label-uppercase` and `text-inverse-secondary`.
- Hero title: `display-hero` or `display-hero-compact` and `text-inverse`.
- Hamilton accent: `accent-script` and `text-inverse`.
- Supporting copy: `body-lg` and `grayscale-100` or `text-inverse-secondary`.
- Primary button on dark: `background`, `text-primary`.
- Secondary button on dark: glass background, `border-dark`, `text-inverse`.

Style rules:

The hero image must cover the full container. Never use a split hero. The subject should sit center-right or right when text is left-aligned. The left side must have enough negative space or overlay strength for readability. The overlay is not optional.

The approved Homepage hero is the benchmark for editorial image treatment, dark overlay behavior, premium contrast, calm luxury mood, and strong typographic hierarchy. Do not copy its exact layout into every internal section; use it as a quality reference.

Social proof pill is optional but recommended for Homepage and Tier 1 landings. It uses Poppins Light, small text, sentence case, line icon, translucent border, glass background, and pill radius.

The Hero H1 must be the dominant page element. It uses uppercase Poppins ExtraBold with compact line-height and negative tracking. Hamilton is allowed only on one inline word.

CTA Group uses two buttons maximum. Primary and secondary buttons must remain sentence case. Icons are small and aligned to the text.

Responsive behavior:

On mobile, preserve the face/treatment subject in the image crop, strengthen overlay, stack CTA buttons, and keep text left-aligned. TrustBar may move below the hero content.

### TrustBar — Immediate Confidence System

Purpose: create immediate trust after the Hero. It is a sibling to HeroSection, not an unrelated content section.

Default items:

1. Seguridad — Protocolos certificados.
2. Calidad — Productos profesionales.
3. Resultados — Tecnología avanzada.
4. ES / EN — Atención bilingüe.

Internal hierarchy:

1. Label.
2. Icon.
3. Title.

Token mapping:

- Label: `label-uppercase`.
- Title: `body-md` with medium weight.
- Card radius: `rounded.card-soft`.
- Dark background: `primary`.
- Dark card: `rgba(255,255,255,0.04)`.
- Light card: `surface` or translucent white.

Dark variant is used directly after a dark Hero. Light/soft variant is used inside light sections. Icons must be linear, monochrome, and not filled.

Labels stay uppercase. Titles stay sentence case. Do not add accent colors.

### FeaturedTreatmentsGrid — Light Editorial Cards

Purpose: introduce the three core protocols and orient the visitor without creating decision overload.

Internal hierarchy:

1. Section eyebrow.
2. Section title.
3. Supporting copy.
4. Three treatment cards.
5. Each card: image, image pill, editorial number, treatment title, description, editorial link.

Token mapping:

- Section background: `background`.
- Section eyebrow: `label-uppercase` and `text-muted`.
- Section title: `heading-section`.
- Supporting copy: `body-lg` or `body-md`.
- Card background: `surface`.
- Card border: `border`.
- Card radius: `rounded.card`.
- Card title: `title-card`.
- Card body: `body-md`.
- Editorial link: `link-editorial`.

Card images use `4:3`, `object-fit: cover`, and sit at the top of the card. The image pill sits bottom-left over the image with glass effect, small Poppins Light or Regular, sentence case, and no uppercase. Treatment/service cards should remain image-led, with white content bodies, `rounded.card`, soft monochrome borders, subtle shadows, and a calm hover state that may gently lift the card or scale the inner image without bouncy motion.

The card link should not look like a button. It is a clean editorial action with a small chevron.

Do not use Hamilton in this section. Do not uppercase card titles or card CTAs. Only the section label and editorial number use uppercase tracking.

### FormComponent

Purpose: capture leads without adding friction.

Inputs use `surface`, `border-strong`, `rounded.control`, and `body-md`. Every input must have a visible label. Do not rely only on placeholders. Error states must include text, not color alone.

Recommended fields:

1. Name.
2. Phone.
3. Email only if needed.
4. Treatment interest.
5. Message.

### FAQAccordion

Purpose: reduce hesitation and support SEO / AEO.

Questions use sentence case and Poppins Medium. Answers use body text with relaxed line-height. Tier 1 FAQs must match real long-tail treatment questions. Do not use generic FAQ.

### FinalCTA — Closing Conversion System

Purpose: close the page with one focused next step.

Internal hierarchy:

1. Eyebrow.
2. Heading.
3. Supporting paragraph.
4. CTA Group.
5. Location or trust context.

CTA must match page intent:

Dark variant is recommended by default. Light variant is allowed if the page already has too much dark contrast.

When a FinalCTA uses an image background, prioritize local contrast inside the content panel. The approved Home FinalCTA uses a dark charcoal glass panel with `text-inverse` heading treatment and `text-inverse-secondary` supporting copy. Do not make the panel so light that text loses contrast. Do not globally over-darken or over-lighten the image if the readability problem can be solved by the panel.


### Footer - Premium Closing System

Token mapping:

- Secondary/footer copy: `text-inverse-secondary`.
- Borders: `border-dark`.
- Logo: approved SVG asset, not live decorative typography.

The approved dark footer is part of the Homepage baseline and should not be reverted to a light footer without explicit approval. Footer links, social icons, contact details, and language controls should stay monochrome, readable, and consistent with the dark surface system.

## Do's and Don'ts

1. Do use full-bleed image or video heroes with mandatory overlays.
2. Don't use split heroes with image on one side and text on the other.
3. Do use photography as the main source of warmth and harmony.
4. Don't use large flat gray sections repeatedly as visual filler.
5. Do use Poppins tokens by semantic role: HeroTitle, SectionTitle, CardTitle, BodyCopy, ButtonLabel.
6. Don't style generic `h1`, `h2`, or `h3` arbitrarily without component context.
7. Do use Hamilton only once inside a Hero H1, or once in an explicitly approved major editorial CTA composition such as the Home FinalCTA.
8. Don't use Hamilton in cards, section headings, body, ordinary buttons, nav, FAQ, stats, forms, treatment titles, or footer.
9. Do keep Hero and section-level headings uppercase when assigned to display roles.
10. Don't uppercase card titles, buttons, body copy, FAQ questions, or links.
11. Do use large radius consistently: `2rem` for cards and `2.5rem` for large sections.
12. Don't use sharp cards, heavy shadows, neon glows, or colorful UI accents.
13. Do keep treatment cards image-led with clean white bodies.
14. Don't use gray placeholders in final UI.
15. Do prioritize WhatsApp in contact flows and Square in direct booking flows.
16. Don't force Post-op visitors into direct booking; route them to conversation first.
17. Do use dark sections intentionally for Hero, TrustBar, protocols, final CTA, footer, and selected proof/trust moments.
18. Don't alternate dark and light sections randomly.
19. Do use white as a content surface for cards, forms, FAQ items, and panels; don't use pure white as the repeated dominant page background.
20. Do resolve FinalCTA contrast locally with panel/overlay strategy; don't wash out the entire background image.
21. Do preserve the approved Homepage baseline during visual consistency passes; don't redesign approved sections unless explicitly asked.
22. Do use semantic HTML, visible labels, accessible focus states, and responsive images.
23. Don't use images of text for real website content.
24. Do use `HealthAndBeautyBusiness`, `LocalBusiness`, `FAQPage`, `BreadcrumbList`, `WebSite`, and `Organization` schema where appropriate.
25. Don't use `MedicalClinic` schema unless legally and strategically approved.
